Atmospheric Scientist · University of Strathclyde

Sandeep Kodoli, PhD

I estimate where a city's CO₂ comes from by combining low-cost sensor measurements with atmospheric transport models. At Strathclyde I lead the urban emissions work in the GEMINOA project, using the BEACO₂N sensor network, WRF–STILT footprints and Bayesian inversion to map Glasgow's emissions at city and neighbourhood scale. Before this, I spent several years measuring black carbon on a Himalayan glacier.

Research

Two threads run through my work: measuring what is in the air, and working backwards from those measurements to where it came from.

2023 – present · Glasgow

Urban CO₂ emissions from sensor networks

I run the city-scale CO₂ inversion for Glasgow, working with ICLEI – Local Governments for Sustainability, UC Berkeley and Glasgow City Council.

  • WRF–STILT modelling chain at 300 m to 3 km resolution, producing hourly footprints
  • Bayesian inversion combining BEACO₂N observations, footprints and prior inventories
  • Low Emission Zone analysis, network design and model configuration for lower uncertainty

2015 – 2023 · Central Himalaya

Black carbon and aerosols over Himalayan glaciers

For my PhD at IITM Pune I set up and ran a high-altitude station on Satopanth Glacier to measure black carbon and other aerosol properties.

  • Separated local biomass burning from long-range transport from the Indo-Gangetic Plain
  • Estimated aerosol direct radiative forcing and atmospheric heating rates
  • Combined ground observations with satellite and reanalysis data

Fieldwork on Satopanth Glacier

Running an observatory at around 4,000 m meant carrying every instrument, battery and box up the valley by foot, then keeping it all working in a tent through the season.
